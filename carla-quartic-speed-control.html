From 0 to 50 km/h: quartic speed planning in CARLA

Yali Nie · October 14, 2026 · CARLA 0.9.16 / Town04

A closed-loop driving experiment combining fourth-order polynomial velocity references, a longitudinal PID controller, Pure Pursuit lateral tracking, a real-time driving HUD, and video capture. The current focus is understanding speed-tracking oscillations during braking—not claiming production-ready control.

Watch the recorded experiment: CARLA Town04 driving demo ↗ (28.55 s; 1280×720, 20 FPS). The recording includes the camera, HUD, and reference/actual speed plot.

1. Quartic reference-speed profile

With normalized time s = clamp(t/T, 0, 1), the reference is:

v_ref = v0 + (v1 - v0) * (4*s**3 - 3*s**4)

The profile changes speed smoothly with zero acceleration at each segment endpoint. Acceleration targets 50 km/h (13.89 m/s), followed by cruising and quartic deceleration to zero. Safety overrides can change the command away from the nominal polynomial when stopping constraints require it.

For braking from speed v0 to zero, the analytically integrated distance is D = 0.4 * v0 * T. We found and corrected a previous implementation that used 0.8 * v0 * T, overestimating the planned braking distance and triggering deceleration too early.

2. Longitudinal PID + lateral Pure Pursuit

The PID controller converts speed error to throttle or brake commands. The controller includes an integral term and anti-windup logic, though separate P/I/D contribution logging is not yet available to verify the integrator’s behavior under braking saturation.

Pure Pursuit computes steering from a lookahead target on the route. The HUD displays a signed lateral deviation relative to the planned route. This is a useful tracking diagnostic, but the vehicle center-of-gravity reference remains an approximation rather than a calibrated physical CoG measurement.

3. Video observations and unresolved questions

The recording shows the vehicle accelerate toward approximately 48 km/h, then decelerate. The two HUD curves are reference speed and actual vehicle speed—not front/rear axle speeds.

MomentObservation
StartVehicle initially stationary
Acceleration / cruiseMeasured speed approaches roughly 48 km/h
About 25 sReference speed ~41.4 km/h, actual ~28 km/h, displayed throttle 100%
Final secondsVehicle slows toward zero; braking command appears in the HUD

The large fluctuations in measured speed during deceleration require further investigation. Candidate explanations include PID tuning, throttle/brake switching, and changing reference limits. Wheel lock-up cannot be established from this recording. We have not logged individual wheel angular velocities or slip ratios, and CARLA’s built-in tire dynamics should not be presented as a validated high-fidelity tire model.

In an earlier run, the vehicle stopped with about 45 m of route remaining because its time-based braking reference reached zero ahead of its spatial destination. The distance integration was corrected and a low-speed recovery mode was introduced, but this revised behavior still requires a fresh end-to-end test.

4. What comes next

  • Validate the full 0 → 50 → 0 km/h cycle and stopping-point accuracy.
  • Log the individual PID P, I and D contributions, and control saturation flags.
  • Measure reference-speed tracking MAE, RMSE and maximum error.
  • Measure signed lateral-error RMSE and maximum absolute deviation.
  • Investigate wheel slip only if suitable wheel-speed data becomes accessible.
  • Publish an updated driving video after closed-loop braking is stabilized.

Development note: This is a learning and engineering experiment. Features are implemented, but the latest quartic-braking fixes and final stopping accuracy are still being evaluated in CARLA.
