How do LiDAR points project onto a camera image?

Yali Nie · September 2026

Projection has two parts: use extrinsic calibration to express a point in the camera frame, then use camera intrinsics to turn that 3D point into a pixel location.

1. Establish the coordinate frames

Let a LiDAR point be p_L = [x, y, z, 1]ᵀ. The extrinsic transform T_C←L maps it into the camera frame: p_C = T_C←L · p_L. If the calibration file provides the transform in the opposite direction, invert it first.

2. Project into the image

For a rectified pinhole camera, let p_C = [X, Y, Z]ᵀ and let K be the camera intrinsic matrix. Then [u', v', w']ᵀ = K · p_C, and the pixel coordinates are (u, v) = (u'/w', v'/w'). Keep points with Z > 0 whose pixels fall inside the image. An unrectified image needs its corresponding distortion model; do not apply rectified intrinsics directly.

3. Account for KITTI's calibration chain

Follow the KITTI raw data calibration notes. A typical chain also includes camera rectification and the selected camera’s projection matrix. Check whether your images are raw or rectified and whether the calibration comes from the raw or object detection dataset before choosing matrices.

A practical check is to draw projected points over the image. They should follow vehicle edges, curbs, and building outlines. If most points are displaced, inspect transform direction, camera index, image dimensions, and timestamp alignment.
